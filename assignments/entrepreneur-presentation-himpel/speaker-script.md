# "Tell me about an entrepreneur!" — HIMPEL / Kim Jung-hwan

5분 팀 발표 스크립트 (영어 원고 + 한글 참고) — 슬라이드는 `HIMPEL_entrepreneur_presentation.pptx` (3장)에 speaker notes로도 들어있습니다.

## Opening (~25초)

> Hello everyone. Our team selected Kim Jung-hwan, the founder of HIMPEL, a Korean ventilation appliance company.
> We selected him because his story shows an important entrepreneurial lesson: he did not create a completely new human need. Instead, he found an old problem that most companies did not take seriously — poor air quality in bathrooms and homes.
> I will explain his journey through three questions: the customer problem, the key entrepreneurial decision, and the connection to our pre-reading.

## Slide 1 — Customer Problem (~80초)

> First — what customer problem did he try to solve?
> In the past, bathroom ventilation fans were treated as cheap building materials. The buyer was often a construction company, but the actual user was the person living in the home. Many users experienced weak ventilation, noise, moisture, odors, and air coming back through the duct.
> So the real customer problem was not simply "we need a fan." The real problem was "we need a clean, dry, quiet, and comfortable bathroom."
> This is an important problem-solving point: instead of asking "how can we make a better fan," HIMPEL could ask "how can we improve the customer's bathroom experience." That wider problem definition created more room for innovation.

**(한글 요약: 환풍기는 저가 건축자재로 취급됐지만, 실제 문제는 "환풍기가 필요하다"가 아니라 "쾌적한 욕실이 필요하다"는 것 — 제품 중심 질문에서 고객 문제 중심 질문으로 전환.)**

## Slide 2 — Entrepreneurial Decision (~100초)

> Second — what entrepreneurial decision made a major difference?
> Kim Jung-hwan originally started a company that produced furniture-related parts. In 1993 he decided to shift the whole business toward ventilation, because he believed indoor air quality could improve people's quality of life. That was an important strategic pivot.
> The company also chose not to compete only through low prices. It invested in technology for consistent airflow, lower noise, and preventing odors and air from flowing backward through the duct.
> But the most important decision was changing the meaning of the product itself: HIMPEL repositioned the ventilation fan from a simple building component to a ventilation appliance. Later, products like Hugent combined ventilation, heating, dehumidifying, and drying in one device.
> Today HIMPEL holds about 60 percent of Korea's bathroom ventilation-fan market. In my opinion, this category redefinition — not the fan itself — was the entrepreneurial action that made the biggest difference. HIMPEL was no longer selling a fan; it was selling a better bathroom experience.

**(한글 요약: 1989년 가구부품 → 1993년 환기사업 피벗 → 카테고리 자체를 "건축자재"에서 "환기가전"으로 재정의. 현재 국내 욕실 환풍기 시장 60% 점유.)**

## Slide 3 — Connection to Pre-Reading (~80초)

> Finally — which idea from the pre-reading best relates to this journey?
> We chose the idea of avoiding poor product-market fit from the Failure Museum reading. That reading explains that companies fail when they build a solution before confirming a compelling customer problem — the product ends up being a nice-to-have instead of a must-have, a solution in search of a problem.
> HIMPEL's journey shows the opposite path. The company focused on a real, repeated problem. It pivoted away from furniture parts into ventilation. It improved the product around actual usage conditions. And it only expanded into new categories after proving strong value in one.

## Conclusion (~20초)

> To conclude: Kim Jung-hwan's main achievement was not simply building a large ventilation company. He saw value in a problem other companies considered too ordinary. His story teaches us that entrepreneurship is the ability to redefine an overlooked problem and create a solution customers truly value. Thank you.

## 예상 질문 대비 (Q&A)

**"Was HIMPEL really a startup?"**
> HIMPEL may no longer be a startup today, but the founder's early journey was entrepreneurial — he pivoted business direction, focused on an underserved problem, developed differentiated technology, and created a new product category.

**"What was the biggest risk?"**
> The biggest risk was investing in a category customers did not yet consider important. Ventilation fans were seen as low-value building components, so HIMPEL had to improve both the product and customer awareness.

**"Was the problem B2B or B2C?"**
> Both. Construction companies were the paying customers, but residents were the actual end users. HIMPEL needed to satisfy technical requirements for B2B buyers and daily-life needs for B2C users.

**주의:** 김정환 대표가 "체계적으로 고객 인터뷰를 진행했다"고 단정하지 말 것 — 공개 자료로는 확인 불가. 대신: *"Based on the company's business transition and product development, we interpret HIMPEL's journey as a customer-problem-driven approach."*
